Online, Everything Helpful Is Missing

Face to face you get a voice, a face, and a pause. In a message you get none of it.

So the person reading fills the gap themselves — with whatever mood they happen to be in.

Learn It Being Good at This

Digital citizenship means behaving well in a place where you cannot see anybody's face.

It is harder than being decent in person, for four reasons.

What is missingWhat that causes
No tone of voiceA joke reads as an insult
No face in front of youIt is easier to be harsher than you would ever be out loud
No pauseYou send before you have thought
It does not go awayA message can be screenshotted and kept for years

Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying is using messages, posts, photos, or group chats to hurt, frighten, or humiliate somebody.

It is not the same as a disagreement, and it is not the same as one unkind remark that gets apologised for. It usually keeps going, and it usually has an audience.

If It Happens to You

  • Do not reply. A reply is what the person is hoping for.
  • Keep it. Screenshot it, even though you want it gone. It is proof.
  • Block or report if the app lets you.
  • Tell an adult you trust — a parent, a teacher, a coach, an older relative.

Telling Someone Is Not Telling Tales

Some people worry that reporting it will make things worse or get them called a snitch.

Telling a trusted adult about someone being hurt is not the same as getting somebody into trouble for nothing. Adults have ways to help that you do not have.

The third person in the room

Most online unkindness has three parts — the person doing it, the person it is aimed at, and everybody watching.

That last group is the bystander, and it is usually the biggest.

⚠ Doing Nothing Is a Choice

Laughing, sharing, adding an emoji, or just leaving it there all tell the person doing it that this is fine.

You do not have to fight anybody. Sending one private message to the person being targeted — that was not okay, are you alright? — changes the whole thing.

Working well with your own team

The same thing applies to how you build robots together.

  • Give proper attribution for code that is not yours. Somebody wrote it, and it is their intellectual property.
  • Find the goal you share, and work toward that rather than toward being right.
  • Keep everybody safe — with tools, with the robot, and with what you say.

Think about it

Your partner read one of your emojis COMPLETELY differently. If that can happen with a drawing you made on purpose, what does it say about sending things quickly?

Most people watching unkindness online do nothing at all. It is not that they approve. So why is it so hard to be the one who says something?

Your team agreement only matters on the day somebody breaks it. What makes a team actually keep to rules it wrote itself?
