No Single Clue Gave It Away

Hair colour on its own tells you almost nothing. Neither does a town, or a school, or a birthday.

Put four of them together and there is usually only one person left. That is how information about you works. Each piece looks harmless on its own.

Learn It What to Keep, and How

Personal information is anything that could be used to work out who you are, where you are, or how to reach you.

Usually safe to share

Things that describe your taste, not your life

Keep to yourself

Things that lead someone to you

Four rules that cover most of it

  • Share private things only with people you actually know and trust.
  • Never post anything you would mind being seen by everybody.
  • Do not over-share. Fewer clues is always safer.
  • Do not post where you are. Not your address, and not a photo that shows it.

The Internet Does Not Forget

Something deleted has usually already been copied, saved, or screenshotted by somebody. The safest moment to protect information is before you post it.

Phishing

Phishing is when a message pretends to be from someone you trust so that you hand over information without thinking.

It usually looks like an email or a message from a real company, a game, or even a friend. The signs are nearly always the same.

Warning signWhy it works
"Act now or your account closes"Panic stops people checking
"You have won a prize"Excitement stops people checking
Asks for a password or a codeNo real company ever does this
Spelling mistakes, odd addressReal companies proofread
A link that looks nearly rightOne changed letter is easy to miss

⚠ The One Rule That Always Works

If a message asks for private information, do not answer it — even if it looks real. Show it to a parent or your teacher instead.

You will never get in trouble for checking with an adult first.

Passwords

A password is the lock on everything else. A weak one takes seconds to guess.

12345
The first thing anyone tries. Guessed instantly.
password
The second thing anyone tries.
Rufus2015
Your dog's name and your birth year are both findable.
Blue7Camel!Sock
Long, mixed, and means nothing to anybody else.
  • Long beats complicated. Four odd words together are stronger than one word with a 3 in it.
  • Mix letters, numbers, and symbols.
  • Do not reuse one password everywhere. One break-in then opens everything.
  • Do not use anything someone could look up — pet, birthday, team, town.

⚠ This Includes Your Robot

Your Wombat has a password. Do not share it outside your team unless your teacher says so. Somebody with it could change or delete every program you have written.

Think about it

In Try It, no single clue identified anyone. Why does that make sharing "just one small thing" harder to judge than it sounds?

A phishing message works by making you feel something — panic, excitement, sympathy. Why do those feelings make people skip checking?

You built a strong password and then had to remember it. What is the trade between safe and easy, and where would you draw the line?
